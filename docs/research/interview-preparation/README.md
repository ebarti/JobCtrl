# Interview preparation content library

Research draft, revised 1 October 2026. This library defines interview questions,
strong-answer guidance, profile adaptations, and evaluation criteria for later
practice. It does not change the current InterviewPrep generator or enable a
practice feature. The numeric anchors are editorial proposals, not validated
predictors of hiring outcomes.

## What makes an answer good?

A good answer addresses the question, makes the candidate's actual contribution
understandable, explains judgment in context, and uses honest evidence. For an
experience question, the listener should understand the situation, the choice,
the action, what happened, and what changed afterward. A disappointing outcome
can accompany a strong answer; a successful outcome can conceal poor judgment.

There is no single ideal leadership persona or universal script. A candidate
should be able to explain a defensible choice and its limits. Different company
needs can make different choices appropriate. These are our working criteria,
derived from the [research](sources.md), rather than a rubric endorsed by the
authors.

All 121 cards contain an intent, answer target, personalization inputs, acceptable
alternatives, probes, failure modes, question-specific anchors, and provenance.
They span 15 topics. The original 40 were a starter set and the next 80 expanded
topic coverage. The content review retired one logistics card and added two
explicit decision-quality questions. Counts are an inventory, not an acceptance
target. Variants and role adaptations do not count as extra cards.
The [coverage map](coverage.md) explains adjacent topics and selection.
These are reusable question families, not a claim to cover every employer's loop.
Technical coding, system-design exercises, industry-specific expertise, and legal
eligibility advice require separate content.

## Personalize to the actual profile and role

Select evidence before drafting prose. Use the role's responsibilities rather
than equating a title or headcount with seniority. An early-career candidate can
use a smaller project with clear ownership; someone changing tracks can state
the boundary between transferable experience and work they have not yet done.

| Target | Emphasize in the same generic question | Do not manufacture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual contributor (IC) | Personal decisions, collaboration, delivery, and learning within actual scope | Organization ownership from participation in a large project |
| Staff/principal IC | Technical direction, ambiguous work across teams, adoption, and enabling others | Direct reports, hiring authority, or people-management experience |
| Engineering manager (EM) | Team outcomes, expectations, coaching, delivery, and work through others | Personal authorship of every team result |
| Director / manager of managers | Multiple teams, manager development, dependencies, and durable operating practices | Executive budget or business authority the candidate did not have |
| Executive | Business context, resource allocation, organizational systems, peer alignment, and accountability | Revenue causality, transformation success, or financial authority without evidence |

Preferences, availability, compensation boundaries, and private career context
are user-owned choices. They are not leadership competencies to grade. Profile
facts, new recollections, hypothetical plans, and evaluator interpretations must
remain distinct; see [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## Suggested first preparation set

Start with C01–C03, C05, C09–C10, B01–B04, and B06. Add M03/M05/M06/M09 for
management, T01/T02/T04/T05 for staff ICs, or E01–E05 for executives. Ask the
recruiter what the actual loop evaluates before selecting the rest. A first-time
manager should also prepare M01 and explicitly identify transferable examples.

Build a small evidence inventory rather than memorizing the whole bank. Each episode
can serve several questions, but the selected angle must answer the question
being asked. Practice follow-ups that expose ownership and reasoning; polishing
an unsupported story does not make it usable.

Also prepare explicit criteria for judgment: B11 asks what makes a decision
good; TS09 applies that question to technical choices. A principle answer needs
a defensible position, its limits, and an example or counterexample. A story
alone does not necessarily explain the principle. C07 defaults to obtaining the
employer's budgeted range before volunteering a candidate number; it does not
grade the person's salary requirement or willingness to risk an opportunity.

## What still needs validation

The library is ready for content review and rehearsal design. Before automated
grading is presented as reliable, experienced interviewers need to calibrate
anchors against independently written responses, diverse communication styles,
and actual role expectations. [Evaluation](evaluation.md#calibration-before-product-use)
defines that work. No candidate responses or private profile facts are included
in this repository content.
