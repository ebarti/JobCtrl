# Personal leadership and career judgment

These questions assess actions, reflection, and decisions. They do not grade
private life choices, health, willingness to overwork, or commitment to a
particular career track. Use [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## LQ01 — How do you manage up when priorities or expectations are unclear?

**Variants:** Your manager gives conflicting instructions.

**Intent:** Examine clarification and influence within actual authority.

**Strong answer target:** Identify the ambiguity and consequences with concrete
examples. Ask about intended outcomes and constraints, offer feasible choices,
and agree a usable priority or decision path. Make unresolved uncertainty visible
and keep your manager informed without requiring them to solve every detail.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Instructions, relationship, authority, choices,
and outcome. IC: bounded priority clarification. Leader: broader mandate and
cross-functional constraints.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Act on an explicit temporary assumption when
delay is costly and authority permits. Escalation can fit a material unresolved risk.

**Probes:** What would you ask first? What can you decide while awaiting clarity?

**Failure modes:** Guessing silently, dismissing the manager as incompetent,
or escalating before attempting proportionate clarification.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambiguity | Complains without identifying the conflict | Specifies expectations and practical consequences |
| Influence | Demands clarity without offering useful context | Presents evidence and feasible choices respectfully |
| Agreement | Continues under incompatible assumptions | Establishes priorities or explicit interim boundaries |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from constraints and influence in [H05](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## LQ02 — How do you work with a manager whose decisions are causing problems?

**Variants:** Tell me about disagreeing with a manager over a recurring issue.

**Intent:** Examine specific feedback, responsibility, and realistic options.

**Strong answer target:** Describe observable decisions and their work impact,
including what you may not know. Seek context, offer evidence and feedback
through a suitable channel, and choose feasible next steps. Explain boundaries
or appropriate escalation when the issue persists or exceeds your role.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Behavior, stakes, relationship, attempts, and
outcomes. Any level can provide a bounded example. Do not require candidates
to diagnose, reform, or remain indefinitely under a difficult manager.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Adjust collaboration, seek mediation, change roles,
or leave. Serious conduct concerns need a different path from ordinary disagreement.

**Probes:** What information might you lack? Which part was within your influence?

**Failure modes:** Character attacks, blind obedience, or portraying resignation
as proof the candidate lacked resilience.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specificity | Labels the manager without concrete impact | Identifies decisions, consequences, and uncertainty |
| Response | Offers only blame or submission | Uses appropriate evidence, dialogue, and feasible choices |
| Boundaries | Assumes the candidate must fix everything | Recognizes authority limits and proportionate next steps |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis informed by [H01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders) and [H05](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders); no assessment of the absent manager's character.

## LQ03 — How do you keep your work sustainable when demands exceed capacity?

**Variants:** What do you do when you cannot responsibly take on more?

**Intent:** Examine boundaries and workload decisions, not private health.

**Strong answer target:** Identify overload early using practical signals,
clarify essential commitments, and communicate feasible tradeoffs. Stop, defer,
delegate, or renegotiate work with appropriate owners. Explain how the arrangement
became sustainable rather than treating repeated personal sacrifice as success.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Workload, choices, support, and observed effect.
IC: personal commitments. Manager: own and team capacity. No diagnosis, family
detail, or health disclosure is required.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Short exceptional periods can occur with explicit
limits and recovery. Boundaries vary; no preferred personal schedule is graded.

**Probes:** What did you stop? How did stakeholders learn what would change?

**Failure modes:** Endless overtime as the only plan, unowned delegation,
or judging a candidate's boundary as insufficient dedication.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Ignores overload until commitments collapse | Recognizes limits with concrete workload signals |
| Tradeoff | Promises all work through more effort | Makes feasible priority and support decisions |
| Sustainability | Depends on continuing exceptional sacrifice | Establishes workable commitments and review conditions |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [R05](sources.md#michael-lopp) and [H04](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## LQ04 — How do you seek support and challenge your own thinking?

**Variants:** Who helps you notice blind spots in your leadership?

**Intent:** Examine useful feedback relationships and independent judgment.

**Strong answer target:** Describe how you obtain relevant challenge through
peers, mentors, specialists, or feedback. Explain a specific insight, how you
tested or used it, and where you retained responsibility for the decision.
Protect confidential information when seeking external advice.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Support sources, advice, action, and effects.
Early-career candidates can use peers; executives can use appropriately scoped
advisors. Famous mentors or paid coaching are not required.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Informal relationships, written reflection, or
structured review can help. Advice can be rejected for a reason.

**Probes:** What challenge changed your behavior? What advice did you decide not to follow?

**Failure modes:** Name-dropping, seeking agreement only, outsourcing judgment,
or sharing private employee/customer material indiscriminately.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Challenge | Describes admiration without useful feedback | Seeks perspectives relevant to a real blind spot |
| Use | Cannot show any consequence of advice | Tests or applies insight with concrete behavior |
| Responsibility | Treats advice as authority to act | Retains judgment and respects information boundaries |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis informed by candid support in [R05](sources.md#michael-lopp) and reflection in [R02](sources.md#michael-lopp).

## LQ05 — How do you learn when your role moves into unfamiliar territory?

**Variants:** Tell me about becoming responsible for work you had not done before.

**Intent:** Examine a practical learning process and honest limits.

**Strong answer target:** State the new responsibility, essential gaps, and
what competent work requires. Learn from relevant people and actual artifacts,
practice on bounded work, and obtain feedback. Explain a changed decision or
capability without claiming mastery from superficial exposure.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Domain, mandate, learning actions, and observations.
IC: unfamiliar system. Manager: new leadership responsibility. Executive:
business or functional context with qualified partners.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Hire or partner with expertise rather than becoming
the expert. Some gaps require a narrower mandate or more time.

**Probes:** What did you initially misunderstand? How did you know your understanding improved?

**Failure modes:** Bluffing, course completion as proof of capability, or
delegating without understanding enough to oversee the responsibility.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gap awareness | Claims immediate competence | Identifies relevant unknowns and responsibility boundaries |
| Learning | Lists resources without practice or feedback | Uses suitable expertise, artifacts, and bounded application |
| Capability | Equates exposure with mastery | Shows a practical improvement and remaining limits |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from changing role demands in [L14](sources.md#will-larson).

## LQ06 — How have you repaired trust after breaking a commitment?

**Variants:** You promised something you could not deliver; what happened next?

**Intent:** Examine relationship repair, distinct from analyzing the project failure.

**Strong answer target:** Name the commitment and effect on others, acknowledge
your contribution, and communicate promptly without excuses. Agree feasible
repair and future expectations. Explain subsequent behavior and any evidence
of restored working trust without asserting the other person forgave you.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Promise, responsibility, affected people, actions,
and subsequent observations. Match the commitment to actual role; personal
accountability does not imply sole responsibility for every outcome.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Trust may remain damaged despite appropriate action.
An honest unresolved outcome can still demonstrate responsible repair.

**Probes:** What did you say? What changed in your later commitments?

**Failure modes:** Apology without action, blaming others, or declaring trust
restored solely because the candidate feels better.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledgment | Minimizes the broken commitment | Recognizes specific responsibility and impact |
| Repair | Offers reassurance without feasible action | Agrees concrete repair and realistic future expectations |
| Follow-through | Treats apology as full restoration | Shows changed behavior and bounds claims about trust |

**Provenance:** Editorial repair question informed by concrete feedback in [H01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## LQ07 — How do you decide you are ready for broader responsibility?

**Variants:** How do you grow your scope without becoming a bottleneck?

**Intent:** Examine capability and readiness for additional work, beyond title ambition.

**Strong answer target:** Identify the new responsibility and relevant evidence
from current work. Seek feedback, test suitable scope with support, and explain
what must change in delegation or ownership. State gaps and conditions rather
than equating tenure with readiness.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Desired scope, observed capability, support,
and trial outcomes. IC: greater technical ambiguity. Manager: larger or different
leadership mandate. No obligation to choose management or ever-larger scope.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Deepen expertise, stay in the current role, or
choose a narrower scope. Development is not a contest for maximum headcount.

**Probes:** What new decision could you handle? What would you stop owning personally?

**Failure modes:** Title as evidence, hidden gaps, or accumulating responsibility
while leaving every decision dependent on the candidate.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope clarity | Wants advancement without naming new work | Identifies the responsibility and relevant capability |
| Evidence | Uses tenure or ambition alone | Offers concrete evidence, feedback, and bounded tests |
| Operating change | Adds scope without changing ownership | Plans support, delegation, and limits sustainably |

**Provenance:** Editorial growth synthesis informed by [R02](sources.md#michael-lopp), [H02](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders), and [J01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## LQ08 — Tell me about a consequential career choice and its tradeoffs

**Variants:** Why did you change tracks, take a smaller role, or turn down an opportunity?

**Intent:** Examine deliberate decision-making without grading personal priorities.

**Strong answer target:** Explain the professional choice, relevant priorities,
feasible options, and uncertainty you are comfortable discussing. Describe what
you gained, gave up, and learned. Distinguish expectations at the time from
later outcomes without retroactively making the decision inevitable.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Actual choices and user-owned disclosure boundaries.
Any career stage qualifies. Compensation, location, caregiving, or health details
are optional; the evaluator does not rank those preferences.

**Acceptable alternatives:** IC/management transitions, lateral moves, stability,
or time away can all be valid. No prestigious employer or promotion is required.

**Probes:** What was uncertain then? What would change your choice now?

**Failure modes:** Requiring an ambition narrative, judging private priorities,
or treating nonlinear careers as poor commitment.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision clarity | Gives no understandable professional choice | Describes relevant options and priorities within boundaries |
| Tradeoffs | Presents only benefits or false inevitability | Acknowledges meaningful costs and uncertainty |
| Reflection | Rewrites later knowledge into the original decision | Separates expectations, results, and learning |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from career-track choice in [J01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders) and reflection in [R02](sources.md#michael-lopp); preferences themselves are ungraded.
