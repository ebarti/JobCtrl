# Culture, inclusion, and ethics questions

Evaluate observable decisions and consequences, not identity, personality,
political alignment, or similarity to the interviewer. Principles need concrete
action and limits; use [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## K01 — How do you encourage candor while maintaining high standards?

**Variants:** People are afraid to raise mistakes or disagree with senior colleagues.

**Intent:** Examine psychological safety alongside accountability.

**Strong answer target:** Show how concerns can be raised, how leaders respond,
and how expectations remain clear. Describe a concrete response to disagreement
or a mistake, distinguish inquiry from avoiding consequences, and examine whether
people could contribute information that improved the work.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Team context, actual behavior, authority, and
response. IC: peer interactions. Leader: decision access and consequences.
Do not infer people's internal psychological state from silence alone.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Written, private, or facilitated channels can help.
Not every concern should be debated publicly.

**Probes:** What happened when someone challenged you? Which standards still applied?

**Failure modes:** “My door is open” without usable access, punishment for
good-faith questions, or equating safety with universal agreement.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Assumes everyone can speak equally easily | Creates practical routes for relevant concerns |
| Response | Dismisses or retaliates against challenge | Demonstrates respectful inquiry and useful follow-up |
| Standards | Avoids expectations to preserve comfort | Combines candor with clear work accountability |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [AE01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).

## K02 — Tell me about a change that made participation more inclusive

**Variants:** How have you improved whose input shapes decisions?

**Intent:** Examine inclusion as an operating change rather than a slogan.

**Strong answer target:** Identify a concrete participation barrier using
appropriate evidence. Describe an intervention shaped with affected people,
check whether access or influence improved, and acknowledge remaining limits.
Avoid speaking for an entire group from one person's experience.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Barrier, user/colleague input, action, and observed
effect. IC: review or meeting practice. Leader: operating policy/resources.
No private identity disclosures are required to supply a useful example.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Small access improvements can matter. An unsuccessful
change with honest learning is useful evidence.

**Probes:** How did affected people shape the change? What actually became more accessible?

**Failure modes:** Assuming inclusion from attendance, token consultation,
or requiring marginalized colleagues to do all improvement work.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barrier | Uses generic claims without an operating problem | Identifies a specific obstacle to participation |
| Intervention | Chooses a symbolic action without input | Addresses the barrier with appropriate involvement |
| Effect | Equates intent with success | Examines access/influence changes and continuing limits |

**Provenance:** Editorial inclusion scenario informed by participation in [AE01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).

## K03 — How do you distribute valuable opportunities and enabling work fairly?

**Variants:** The same people receive visible projects while others do the coordination.

**Intent:** Examine access, workload, and career consequences of assignment.

**Strong answer target:** Understand who receives which work and why, including
preferences, readiness, support, and recognition. Address recurring imbalance
through transparent assignment, protected capacity, development, and credit.
Check whether changes improve opportunity without simply rotating unsupported burdens.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Assignment patterns, choice, authority, and
outcomes. IC: raise patterns and share credit. Manager: allocate work and
development. Do not infer discrimination from one assignment alone.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Different preferences and specialization can justify
different work. Enabling work can be valuable when recognized and sustainable.

**Probes:** Who gets time to learn? Does the promotion process value the assigned work?

**Failure modes:** Always asking the helpful person, invisible career penalties,
or nominal rotation without training and support.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern awareness | Assumes assignments have equal consequences | Examines access, burden, and recognition patterns |
| Allocation | Ignores preferences or capability support | Makes defensible assignments with choice and development |
| Consequences | Leaves enabling work invisible | Addresses credit, sustainability, and career effects |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [TR01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## K04 — How do you respond when someone reports harmful behavior?

**Variants:** A colleague says they were excluded or treated disrespectfully.

**Intent:** Examine care, responsible inquiry, and appropriate escalation.

**Strong answer target:** Listen without dismissing or promising an outcome
before understanding facts. Explain confidentiality limits and next steps,
consider immediate protection needs, and involve the appropriate responsible
process. Follow up within your role while avoiding retaliation or public adjudication.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Reported facts, seriousness, authority, and
available channels. IC: support and appropriate reporting. Manager: response
within scope; formal investigations require the responsible specialists.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Different concerns require different paths. A
direct conversation is not mandatory when power or safety makes it unsuitable.

**Probes:** What would you say first? Which promises can you actually keep?

**Failure modes:** Demanding proof before listening, gossip, absolute secrecy,
or protecting a powerful person's reputation over appropriate inquiry.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial care | Dismisses the concern or pressures disclosure | Listens and identifies appropriate immediate needs |
| Process | Predetermines conclusions or exceeds authority | Uses proportionate inquiry and responsible channels |
| Follow-up | Leaves the reporter unsupported or exposed | Explains next steps and monitors relevant ongoing risk |

**Provenance:** Editorial conduct scenario; care and power informed by [U02](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders). Not an investigative procedure.

## K05 — What do you do when stated values conflict with actual rewards?

**Variants:** Collaboration is praised, but promotions reward individual heroics.

**Intent:** Examine incentives as evidence of operating priorities.

**Strong answer target:** Identify a concrete mismatch and its effects. Examine
who controls recognition, allocation, and advancement. Change relevant decisions
within authority or raise the discrepancy with evidence; check whether behavior
and opportunity shift rather than just rewriting values.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Values, actual decisions, role, and intervention.
IC: attribution and raising evidence. Leader: reward/assignment decisions.
Do not assume every inconsistency is deliberate deception.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Some competing values need explicit tradeoffs.
A bounded local correction can help even when broader authority is absent.

**Probes:** Which decision sent the strongest signal? What would you reward differently?

**Failure modes:** More slogans, rewarding repeated rescue while ignoring
prevention, or promising process changes outside authority.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mismatch | Repeats values without examining decisions | Shows a concrete incentive/behavior discrepancy |
| Action | Proposes communications only | Changes or challenges consequential decisions appropriately |
| Effect | Assumes new wording changes culture | Examines resulting behavior and continuing tradeoffs |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from the introductory principle in [H07](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders); reward-system criteria are ours.

## K06 — How do you handle different expectations about working norms?

**Variants:** Colleagues disagree about responsiveness, disagreement, or meeting behavior.

**Intent:** Examine explicit agreements rather than assumed cultural similarity.

**Strong answer target:** Describe concrete behavior and its work impact,
ask about differing expectations, and distinguish necessary requirements from
personal preferences. Agree usable norms with room for legitimate variation
and revisit whether the agreement solves the problem.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Norm conflict, constraints, participation needs,
and authority. Any role can contribute. Do not explain an individual's behavior
using stereotypes about nationality, identity, or personality.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Different teams can use different norms when
interfaces remain clear. Written clarification or facilitated dialogue can fit.

**Probes:** Which norm is essential to the work? What did you learn about their expectations?

**Failure modes:** “Professionalism” as undefined similarity, forced uniformity,
or ignoring constraints because one style feels more familiar.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding | Uses stereotypes or assumed intent | Examines specific behavior and differing expectations |
| Requirement | Treats preference as a universal standard | Separates work needs from optional style |
| Agreement | Requires one-sided adaptation | Establishes workable shared expectations and checks them |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis informed by priorities in [H03](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders) and clear norms in [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).

## K07 — What would you do with a request to use customer data in a questionable way?

**Variants:** A useful experiment needs data access beyond the team's current permissions.

**Intent:** Examine boundaries, evidence, and appropriate risk ownership.

**Strong answer target:** Clarify the purpose, required data, permissions,
expectations, and uncertainty. Seek privacy/security or other responsible
expertise, pause unsupported use, and compare safer ways to meet the objective.
Explain authorization, auditability, and verification without claiming legal expertise.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Requested use, data sensitivity, authority, and
controls. IC: raise concerns and implement permitted alternatives. Leader:
accountable decisions with the responsible owners.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Synthetic, minimized, aggregated, or explicitly
authorized data may fit. No particular technique guarantees appropriateness.

**Probes:** What data is truly needed? Who can authorize the use?

**Failure modes:** Business urgency as permission, informal access sharing,
or assuming de-identification removes all risk.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and scope | Uses all available data by default | Clarifies necessary use, sensitivity, and constraints |
| Authority | Treats technical access as permission | Obtains appropriate responsible review and authorization |
| Alternative | Frames safeguards as blocking the business | Compares feasible safer paths and auditable controls |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [L16](sources.md#will-larson); not a legal compliance checklist.

## K08 — How do you ensure disagreement can improve a decision?

**Variants:** Everyone agrees with the most senior person immediately.

**Intent:** Examine access to decision influence before authority closes discussion.

**Strong answer target:** Invite independent perspectives before anchoring,
make relevant evidence accessible, and seek omitted expertise. Show a case where
dissent changed or refined a choice, or was considered and rejected with reasons.
Clarify closure and continued routes for material new evidence.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Decision, power differences, contribution, and
effect. Leader: create access and avoid premature anchoring. IC: provide evidence
and help others participate without inventing authority.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Time-sensitive decisions may limit discussion.
Listening does not require adopting every objection or perpetual consensus.

**Probes:** Whose input was missing? What happened to the strongest objection?

**Failure modes:** Performative consultation after deciding, requiring dissenters
to be charismatic, or treating objections as personal opposition.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Assumes assent means all expertise was heard | Creates practical routes for independent relevant input |
| Consideration | Ignores objections without engaging evidence | Addresses substantive dissent and explains its effect |
| Closure | Silences new facts or never decides | Reaches accountable closure with a path for material revision |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [R03](sources.md#michael-lopp) and [AE01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).
