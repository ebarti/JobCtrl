# Talent decision questions

Use concrete decisions and the candidate's actual authority. People decisions
need applicable organizational processes and responsible specialists; these are
interview-content rubrics, not employment-law procedures. See [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## HR01 — How do you help a new hire become effective?

**Variants:** What would you change about an onboarding program?

**Intent:** Examine capability, context, and belonging beyond a checklist.

**Strong answer target:** Understand the person's starting context and role.
Establish access, learning goals, support relationships, and meaningful early
work. Clarify ownership and expectations, then use feedback and observed
independence to improve the onboarding experience.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Role, starting needs, support, and outcomes.
IC: buddy or technical onboarding contribution. Manager: expectations and
team support. Director: program ownership across teams.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Different roles can require different pacing.
A useful first contribution need not be a production deployment.

**Probes:** Who noticed when the hire was stuck? How did they learn unwritten context?

**Failure modes:** Measuring completion only by courses attended, no support
owner, or expecting newcomers to infer everything through trial and error.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | Gives every hire an identical checklist | Adapts learning and access to role and starting needs |
| Support | Assumes the hire will find help | Establishes expectations, owners, and useful relationships |
| Effectiveness | Equates attendance with readiness | Examines practical capability and improves gaps |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [L18](sources.md#will-larson).

## HR02 — What do you do when interviewers disagree about a candidate?

**Variants:** A panel strongly likes someone but the evidence is mixed.

**Intent:** Examine a hiring decision anchored to role criteria.

**Strong answer target:** Return to agreed requirements and each interviewer's
specific observations. Separate evidence from impressions and identify missing
or contradictory signals. Apply the agreed decision process, obtaining useful
additional evidence only when appropriate rather than repeatedly testing until consensus.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Hiring mandate, criteria, observations, and
decision role. Interviewer: evidence contribution. Hiring manager: accountable
decision; panel participation alone is not hiring authority.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Hire, decline, or run a focused additional exercise
can fit. Unanimity is not inherently required.

**Probes:** Which requirement is disputed? Is the objection about demonstrated work or similarity?

**Failure modes:** Seniority decides without evidence, “culture fit” as an
unexamined veto, or counting votes instead of assessing criteria.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criteria | Changes requirements to favor a preferred person | Uses relevant agreed role requirements |
| Evidence | Treats enthusiasm as proof | Separates observations, interpretations, and missing signals |
| Decision | Seeks agreement without closure | Uses accountable resolution and proportionate follow-up |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [L01](sources.md#will-larson) and [L02](sources.md#will-larson); our panel scenario.

## HR03 — How do you assess potential when someone lacks direct experience?

**Variants:** Would you hire a first-time manager or someone changing domains?

**Intent:** Examine transferable evidence and the cost of development.

**Strong answer target:** Specify which capabilities must exist on entry and
which can develop. Examine relevant past behavior and suitable situational
work, label remaining uncertainty, and assess available support. Explain a
decision without turning a hypothetical answer into proven experience.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Role requirements, adjacent experience, exercises,
and support capacity. Hiring leader: actual choice. Candidate changing tracks:
honest evidence and development plan rather than invented credentials.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Prefer experience for an urgent unsupported role,
or invest in potential where support and requirements permit.

**Probes:** Which skill transfers? What must be learned, and who will help?

**Failure modes:** Charisma as potential, pedigree as capability, or assuming
all risks disappear with a mentor.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements | Treats all gaps as equally disqualifying or irrelevant | Distinguishes entry needs from learnable capabilities |
| Evidence | Confuses intention with demonstrated behavior | Identifies concrete transfer and residual uncertainty |
| Investment | Offers opportunity without feasible support | Assesses development cost, support, and fit to urgency |

**Provenance:** Direct first-time-manager hiring theme in [F01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders), contrasted with demonstrated skills in [L02](sources.md#will-larson).

## HR04 — How do you make promotion calibration fair across teams?

**Variants:** Teams provide very different opportunities and promotion evidence.

**Intent:** Examine consistency without pretending contexts are identical.

**Strong answer target:** Use role-relevant expectations and concrete evidence.
Compare responsibilities and contribution, not just visibility or project size.
Examine unequal access to opportunities and enabling work, resolve inconsistencies,
and communicate decisions and development needs through appropriate channels.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Calibration role, criteria, opportunity patterns,
and decisions. Manager: evidence and advocacy. Director: consistency across
teams; confidential individual details can remain abstract.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Different role scopes can warrant different outcomes.
A process can be imperfect while specific corrections meaningfully improve it.

**Probes:** Who lacks the chance to demonstrate the criteria? How do you assess less-visible work?

**Failure modes:** Visibility equals impact, retroactive criteria, or penalizing
someone for doing assigned work that the process does not reward.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | Uses reputation or inconsistent expectations | Compares evidence against relevant shared criteria |
| Opportunity | Assumes everyone had equivalent access | Examines context and unequal demonstration opportunities |
| Resolution | Leaves discrepancies unexplained | Makes accountable decisions and addresses process gaps |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [L17](sources.md#will-larson) and [TR01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## HR05 — How do you approach compensation fairness under a limited budget?

**Variants:** You discover unexplained pay differences within your organization.

**Intent:** Examine evidence, tradeoffs, and truthful communication.

**Strong answer target:** Clarify the organization's compensation principles,
relevant data, role scope, and decision authority with responsible specialists.
Investigate discrepancies without assuming their cause. Compare feasible
corrections and budget tradeoffs; explain what is decided, uncertain, or outside your control.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Actual authority, available comparisons, constraints,
and actions. Manager: escalation and clear communication. Executive: budget and
policy accountability. Never invent access to peers' private pay data.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Staged corrections may be necessary. Equal pay
amounts are not automatically the same as a defensible compensation system.

**Probes:** Which differences are explained? What can you truthfully promise?

**Failure modes:** Unsupported promises, treating budget as an excuse never to
investigate, or disclosing others' private compensation.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | Assumes a cause from one comparison | Uses relevant criteria and data with appropriate expertise |
| Tradeoffs | Offers fairness slogans without feasible action | Compares corrections, resources, and continuing constraints |
| Communication | Promises outcomes outside authority | Explains decisions and uncertainty while preserving privacy |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from [L17](sources.md#will-larson); not a compensation or legal formula.

## HR06 — A high performer harms colleagues. How do you respond?

**Variants:** A technically valuable person repeatedly undermines the team.

**Intent:** Examine whether output is allowed to excuse harmful behavior.

**Strong answer target:** Establish specific behavior and impact, support
affected people, and use appropriate fact-finding and organizational processes.
Set clear expectations, follow up, and address recurrence. Explain how technical
dependency will be reduced so it does not prevent accountability.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Observed behavior, authority, responsible partners,
and follow-up. IC: raise concerns and address collaboration within scope.
Manager: expectations; serious issues need the appropriate specialist process.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Coaching, reassignment, or formal action depends
on facts and seriousness. A generic coaching sequence cannot fit every concern.

**Probes:** What if the person is indispensable? How do you protect those reporting concerns?

**Failure modes:** “That's just their style,” trading team safety for output,
or prejudging allegations without a responsible process.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behavior evidence | Uses labels or dismisses concrete harm | Identifies conduct and impact through appropriate inquiry |
| Accountability | Grants an output-based exemption | Applies clear expectations and proportionate follow-up |
| Team protection | Leaves others to absorb harm | Supports affected people and reduces obstructive dependency |

**Provenance:** Editorial scenario informed by behavior/outcome distinctions in [L17](sources.md#will-larson) and care/standards in [U02](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## HR07 — How do you respond when an important team member wants to leave?

**Variants:** How do you retain people without making promises you cannot keep?

**Intent:** Examine respect, useful learning, and continuity.

**Strong answer target:** Listen to the person's goals and constraints without
pressuring disclosure. Explain realistic options and limits, respect their
decision, and arrange proportionate knowledge transfer. Examine whether their
experience reveals a broader problem requiring action.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Relationship, expressed goals, feasible options,
and authority. Manager: direct conversation. Director: systemic patterns.
Do not grade the employee's reasons as more or less loyal.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Supporting departure can be the right outcome.
A counteroffer may fit but is not the default definition of success.

**Probes:** What can you actually change? What would you improve even if they leave?

**Failure modes:** Guilt, retaliation, impossible promises, or treating retention
as winning regardless of the person's interests.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding | Assumes the motivation or demands private detail | Listens to stated needs and respects boundaries |
| Options | Uses pressure or unsupported promises | Offers honest feasible choices and respects the decision |
| Continuity | Blames the leaver for organizational dependency | Plans handover and investigates broader lessons |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis informed by [H03](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders) and growth reflection in [R02](sources.md#michael-lopp).

## HR08 — How would you lead through a significant reduction in team capacity?

**Variants:** Your organization must reduce spending and staffing.

**Intent:** Examine humane accountability and a credible remaining operating plan.

**Strong answer target:** Clarify the business constraints and decision authority.
Work with responsible people/organizational specialists on decisions and communication.
Treat affected people respectfully, protect private information, and reduce
commitments to match remaining capacity. Explain support and operational continuity.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Mandate, constraints, obligations, and role.
Executive: allocation accountability. Manager: implementation and support within
scope. Situational answers cannot imply prior layoff experience.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Other savings, narrower scope, or staged changes
can be considered if feasible. No particular employment procedure is prescribed.

**Probes:** Which commitments stop? What can you communicate truthfully before decisions are final?

**Failure modes:** Expecting unchanged output, casual disclosure of decisions,
or using euphemisms to avoid acknowledging consequences.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accountability | Treats reductions as someone else's problem | Explains constraints, authority, and feasible alternatives |
| Care | Ignores privacy and human consequences | Uses respectful communication and appropriate support/process |
| Operating reset | Expects the same work with less capacity | Reconciles priorities, continuity, and remaining responsibilities |

**Provenance:** Editorial scenario using resource reasoning in [L04](sources.md#will-larson) and sustainable work in [U01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders); not legal guidance.
