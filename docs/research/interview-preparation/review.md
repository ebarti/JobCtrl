# Content review and open judgments

Reviewed 1 October 2026 after the candidate-facing content critique. All 120
original answer targets, question-specific anchors, and failure modes were
examined. This records current editorial judgments, not a reconstruction of
unrecorded doubts during the original research or independent rubric validation.

## What the critique revealed

The library was organized around topic coverage, with much of the definition of
good judgment implicit in experience and scenario answers. Explicit criteria
were uneven: M09 asks what makes a team healthy, O03 asks what makes an incident
review useful, and W04 asks what makes a decision record useful, but there was
no equivalent question about a good decision or a good technical decision.
Logistics also passed the selection boundary despite offering little substantive
preparation value. Salary advice treated several tactics as equally useful
without recommending an information-seeking default.

The repair belongs in question selection and canonical answer guidance. The
atlas copies those sources; changing only its appearance would preserve the
editorial defect. Counts describe coverage and must never become a target that
justifies filler or equal-sized categories.

## Changes made

- Retired C08; availability, location, travel, and authorization remain
  user-owned checklist facts. Its ID is preserved as retired, not recycled.
- Rewrote C07 to seek the employer's budgeted range firmly and persistently
  before volunteering a candidate number. Pressure, vague bands, package
  ambiguity, and refusal now have explicit preparation guidance. The final
  choice remains with the candidate.
- Added B11, “What makes a decision a good decision?”, and TS09, “What makes
  a technical decision a good technical decision?”, with separate criteria
  and application probes.
- Added principle/concept answers to evaluation. A definition question needs
  criteria, limits, and application; it should not be forced into a success story.
- Added synthetic C07 pressure-handling dialogue and B11/TS09 principle answers
  with counterexamples. There are now 17 families with worked illustrations.

## Doubts that remain

These are content review judgments, not claims that the original authors agree.

1. **How much of this bank matters for a particular loop?** M&A integration,
   reductions, incident response, platform design, and agent authority are useful
   in specific roles. They should not make a universal preparation curriculum.
2. **Are neighboring cards genuinely distinct?** Several have different targets
   but can use the same episode. Preserve that distinction in evaluation, while
   avoiding repetitive practice. The walkthrough flags the closest overlaps.
3. **Are principles explicit enough?** Decision quality is now covered. Good
   management, staff-level contribution, engineering quality, and organizational
   effectiveness still deserve an explicit concept-to-example coverage review.
4. **Do the answer targets overprescribe process?** “Context, options, evidence,
   owner, checks” is useful but can become a checklist response. A strong answer
   must choose material criteria and explain a position; every answer need not
   mention every safeguard or prove that a formal process was used.
5. **How much attribution is direct?** Sources include direct interview guidance,
   extrapolated practices, selected passages, and book overviews. Book listings
   are reading paths, not evidence of complete-book research. Our anchors remain
   editorial even when the interview theme comes directly from an author.
6. **Where do authors disagree?** Chemistry versus content, technical involvement,
   potential versus demonstrated work, and deciding versus delegating have real
   tensions. Accountable delegation and abdication must not be conflated. The
   source ledger records the decisions rather than implying consensus.
7. **How trustworthy is evaluation?** Three dimensions per card are a convenient
   draft convention, not evidence that every competency has exactly three parts.
   Only 14 original question families have worked examples. Neither those
   illustrations nor numeric anchors validate automated grading. Independent
   calibration, unconventional sound answers, and unsuccessful sound decisions
   remain necessary before product use.

## Question-by-question walkthrough

“Keep” means useful preparation content, not a mandate to practice it. “Select”
means choose it for actual responsibilities or a known interview. “Overlap”
identifies an adjacent target to keep distinct. A row is an editorial review
note; the owning card contains the full answer and rubric.

## Proposed next content work

These are review proposals, not additional cards already present:

- Explicitly define a good manager and good staff/principal contribution, then
  link those criteria to existing episodes and scenarios.
- Strengthen executive mandate, CEO/board partnership, and financial tradeoffs
  where the target role calls for them.
- Expand the new decision-quality illustrations into independently written
  historical answers and unconventional but sound alternatives. Test C07
  against a disclosed range, persistent refusal, and candidate-chosen exceptions.
- Resolve flagged overlaps using actual interview loops; keep different
  judgment targets even when they share one evidence episode.
- Calibrate the draft anchors independently before using them for automated
  feedback. Do not optimize for a predetermined question count.
