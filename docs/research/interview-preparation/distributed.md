# Distributed work and communication questions

Assess useful communication and operating choices across locations and schedules.
Accent, sociability, camera use, response speed outside agreed hours, and grammar
are not competence proxies. Use [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## W01 — When do you use asynchronous communication and when do you meet?

**Variants:** How do you choose the right communication channel?

**Intent:** Examine communication suited to the work and participants.

**Strong answer target:** Consider urgency, complexity, ambiguity, access,
and the decision needed. Explain how written context supports independent work
and when live discussion helps resolve uncertainty. Capture useful conclusions
so absent colleagues can act.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Work example, participants, constraints, and
outcome. IC: practical channel choices. Leader: norms and access across teams.
No universal remote policy is required.

**Acceptable alternatives:** A live conversation can be best; an asynchronous
default can also work. Sensitive concerns may need private channels.

**Probes:** What would make you switch channels? How do people learn what was decided?

**Failure modes:** Meetings for every update, text-only dogma, or decisions
trapped in a conversation inaccessible to affected people.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel fit | Uses one channel for every need | Chooses based on urgency, ambiguity, and access |
| Context | Requires repeated personal explanation | Provides enough context for useful independent work |
| Conclusion | Discussion has no usable outcome | Records relevant decisions, owners, and next steps |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from selected [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research) communication policies.

## W02 — How do you work fairly across time zones?

**Variants:** One region always attends meetings outside normal hours.

**Intent:** Examine coverage and collaboration without permanent unequal burden.

**Strong answer target:** Identify overlap, required response coverage, and
who bears current inconvenience. Use async work, clear handoffs, and deliberate
scheduling to reduce burden. Agree exceptions for urgent work and examine
whether influence and access remain equitable.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Locations, coverage needs, existing burdens,
and authority. IC: handoff practice. Manager: schedules and expectations.
Do not assume every person can shift personal hours equally.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Regional ownership, rotating live sessions, or
limited overlap can fit. Rotation alone may not solve unequal decision access.

**Probes:** Which work truly requires overlap? Who misses decisions under this arrangement?

**Failure modes:** “Everyone is flexible,” permanent late-night attendance,
or global availability as a performance expectation.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burden | Ignores who absorbs inconvenient hours | Identifies coverage needs and unequal costs |
| Design | Solves coordination through constant availability | Uses feasible ownership, handoffs, and scheduling |
| Access | Equates meeting rotation with fairness | Checks decision influence and continuing burden |

**Provenance:** Editorial distributed-work scenario informed by availability boundaries in [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).

## W03 — How do you prevent proximity bias in a hybrid team?

**Variants:** People near leadership receive more information and opportunities.

**Intent:** Examine location-independent access to consequential work.

**Strong answer target:** Investigate where decisions, recognition, and
assignments depend on physical presence. Make relevant information and participation
accessible, use work evidence in decisions, and check opportunity patterns.
Avoid assuming equal access because remote attendance is technically possible.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Location patterns, work/decision examples,
authority, and effects. IC: document decisions and include peers. Leader:
assignments and evaluation consistency.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Some work legitimately requires a location;
explain the requirement and consequences rather than treating presence as dedication.

**Probes:** What decisions happen in hallway conversations? Who gets high-value work?

**Failure modes:** Visibility as commitment, remote observers without influence,
or rewarding attendance rather than contribution.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern | Assumes video access eliminates disadvantage | Examines information, assignments, and recognition by location |
| Intervention | Requires more visible presence | Improves practical access and evidence-based decisions |
| Verification | Declares fairness from policy alone | Checks actual opportunities and decision participation |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis from communication access in [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research) and opportunity concerns in [TR01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## W04 — What makes a written decision record useful?

**Variants:** How do you communicate a decision to people who were not in the meeting?

**Intent:** Examine actionable context and institutional memory.

**Strong answer target:** Capture the problem, relevant constraints, options,
decision, rationale, owner, and important follow-up. Choose an accessible location,
distinguish approved decisions from proposals, and keep material changes visible.
Write for actual consumers rather than documenting every conversation.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Decision, readers, record, and use. IC: technical
decision. Leader: shared operating choice. Confidential details should have
appropriate access rather than being indiscriminately published.

**Acceptable alternatives:** A short note can suffice. Not every small decision
needs a lengthy RFC or permanent archival process.

**Probes:** Can a newcomer act from the record? How will they know it changed?

**Failure modes:** Transcript dumps, missing ownership, obsolete decisions
treated as current, or inaccessible private-channel conclusions.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | Gives the conclusion without needed rationale | Explains enough problem and constraints for the reader |
| Action | Leaves status and responsibility ambiguous | Identifies decision status, owner, and next steps |
| Accessibility | Assumes everyone heard the original discussion | Provides findable, appropriately accessible current information |

**Provenance:** Practice extrapolation from written conclusions in [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research).

## W05 — How do you decide whether a recurring meeting is useful?

**Variants:** Your team's calendar leaves little time for focused work.

**Intent:** Examine meeting purpose, access, and opportunity cost.

**Strong answer target:** State the meeting's intended outcome and necessary
participants. Examine whether it produces decisions, coordination, or valuable
support; compare alternatives. Change format, attendance, cadence, or existence,
then check effects on both work and participation.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Meeting purpose, participant needs, changes,
and observed effects. IC: propose improvements. Manager: team cadence.
Do not remove listening/support channels merely because they lack status outputs.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Some meetings are valuable for relationships or
sensitive discussion. Smaller, less frequent, or asynchronous formats can fit.

**Probes:** What breaks if it stops? Who can contribute under the new format?

**Failure modes:** Attendance as effectiveness, blanket meeting bans,
or replacing coordination with invisible extra work.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Cannot identify a useful outcome | Clarifies decision, coordination, or support need |
| Cost and access | Optimizes only the organizer's convenience | Examines time, participation, and alternatives |
| Adjustment | Changes cadence without checking consequences | Evaluates whether the revised arrangement still serves the need |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis from [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research) and listening beyond status in [R06](sources.md#michael-lopp).

## W06 — How do you repair a conflict that escalated through remote messages?

**Variants:** A chat exchange is becoming increasingly hostile or ambiguous.

**Intent:** Examine channel limitations and substantive repair.

**Strong answer target:** Review concrete messages and work impact without
assuming intent. Clarify missing context and choose a suitable private, written,
or live conversation. Address the actual disagreement or conduct, agree next
steps, and capture relevant conclusions for affected colleagues.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Messages, relationship, stakes, role, and outcome.
Any role can describe contribution. A synchronous conversation is optional,
especially where access or safety makes it inappropriate.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Clear written clarification, mediation, or escalation
may fit. Not every dispute is merely a tone misunderstanding.

**Probes:** What substantive concern remains after clarification? Why choose that channel?

**Failure modes:** Assuming malicious intent from terse wording, forced calls,
or resolving tone while leaving the work conflict unchanged.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interpretation | Infers motive from style alone | Separates observed messages, impact, and unknown intent |
| Repair channel | Continues escalation or forces an unsuitable format | Chooses an accessible, proportionate clarification path |
| Resolution | Declares peace without addressing the issue | Agrees substantive next steps and usable communication |

**Provenance:** Editorial synthesis informed by observation-based feedback in [H01](sources.md#other-contemporary-leaders).

## W07 — How do you help a remote newcomer build context and working relationships?

**Variants:** A new colleague is technically onboarded but still isolated from the team.

**Intent:** Examine informal context and access beyond setup completion.

**Strong answer target:** Ask what context and connections the newcomer needs.
Provide explicit introductions, a usable help path, and supported collaboration
on meaningful work. Make unwritten norms discoverable and check whether they
can find information and participate without requiring a particular social style.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Newcomer needs, support relationship, actions,
and observations. Buddy: bounded contribution. Manager: ownership and team practices.
Do not diagnose loneliness from quietness alone.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Optional social interaction, pairing, written
orientation, or small group sessions can work; mandatory socializing is unnecessary.

**Probes:** Who can they ask safely? What information was previously available only informally?

**Failure modes:** More tooling as the whole answer, forced camera participation,
or expecting accidental conversations to supply all context.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs | Assumes setup completion establishes connection | Asks about practical context and relationship gaps |
| Support | Leaves introductions and help to chance | Creates accessible relationships and useful collaboration |
| Independence | Measures belonging by sociability | Checks information access and participation without style penalties |

**Provenance:** Editorial remote adaptation of onboarding ownership in [L18](sources.md#will-larson).

## W08 — How do you communicate when people have different language or access needs?

**Variants:** Important decisions are difficult for some colleagues to follow.

**Intent:** Examine comprehension without judging intelligence from presentation.

**Strong answer target:** Ask about usable formats and constraints without
demanding private diagnoses. Provide clear context, explain terms, allow preparation
or written contributions where helpful, and check shared understanding respectfully.
Preserve substantive standards while improving the route to participation.

**Profile inputs/adaptation:** Communication need, preferences, action, and
effect. Any role can adapt interactions; organizational accommodations require
the responsible channel. Do not assume one solution fits everyone.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Plain-language notes, captions, advance material,
or different channels can help when participants want them.

**Probes:** How did you check comprehension without embarrassment? What preference did you ask about?

**Failure modes:** Accent or speed as competence, unsolicited diagnoses,
or simplifying the person's responsibilities instead of improving communication.

| Dimension | Weak anchor (0) | Strong anchor (3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs inquiry | Assumes ability or demands protected detail | Respectfully asks about practical communication needs |
| Adaptation | Requires everyone to use the same format | Provides appropriate accessible ways to understand and contribute |
| Substance | Judges style rather than reasoning | Checks work understanding while preserving relevant expectations |

**Provenance:** Editorial accessibility synthesis informed by clear communication in [GI01](sources.md#additional-topic-research); no medical inference or universal accommodation prescription.
